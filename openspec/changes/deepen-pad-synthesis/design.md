# A held chord as the design brief

A player should be able to follow one sound while another event takes place behind it. For a reference patch, give the bass a stable center, the upper voices a much slower bloom, and a distant texture a life that continues when the keys lift. These movements should unfold with the player's hands resting on the keys. One well-chosen control should let the player bring a particular layer into focus.

## What Blue can already do

Glass Tide uses one global two-point envelope, a short FM decay, two harmonic frames and a frozen granular layer. Its first macro moves morph, cutoff and density together. The engine supports richer per-lane routes than this preset demonstrates. Tideline uses those routes, a moving grain read position and original source material to separate the layers. It is a working starting point for listening decisions. [Held-chord comparison](../../../studies/pad-depth/README.md)

## What to borrow from Absynth 5

Absynth 5 gives individual oscillators amplitude envelopes and lets authors assign breakpoint envelopes to other parameters. Its control-driven envelopes and envelope LFOs let a gesture travel through an authored shape. Its Aetherizer can distribute grains in stereo, shape their pitch and filtering, and route the result through feedback and a post-delay. These are particularly relevant to long pads. [Native Instruments reference manual, sections 6.7 and 8](https://docs.native-instruments.com/pdf-guides/Absynth_Manual_English.pdf)

## Implementation order

1. **Give each layer its own clock and release.** Compile named curves to bounded arrays shared by voices. Keep a per-voice cursor for each active curve. Support note-triggered and transport/free-running time, sustain regions, loop regions and a release that starts continuously from the current value. Let authors choose a shared phase for deliberate ensemble swells or a repeatably seeded start offset per voice for looser motion. Offer curve sources in the matrix for level, tone, morph and grain scan position. Keep the legacy envelope path for old packs. The author should be able to see several contours against the same seconds/beats ruler, drag loop boundaries and audition one lane. First patch brief: a reed reaches full level in two seconds, a choir arrives over eighteen, and a third layer begins its bloom on key release. Demonstrate it dry.
2. **Give grains and oscillators richer material.** Blue currently stores one mono sample of up to 65,536 frames per patch, and a table lane morphs between two frames of up to 16 harmonic amplitudes. Add per-lane clip references into an immutable, byte-budgeted sample pool. Start with two short stereo recordings that change character from beginning to end. Expose scan position, pitch spread, grain duration, envelope shape and stereo position as independent controls. Add multi-frame spectral interpolation after the grain material has passed its listening test. First patch brief: scrub bow noise into a pitched glass resonance without transposing the played chord.
3. **Make the space playable.** Build a bounded stereo granular feedback bus inspired by the Aetherizer. Give grains independent stereo placement, a small interval palette, damping and diffusion. Apply smoothing and energy limits inside feedback. Audition at unity pitch first, then add fifths and octaves. First patch brief: a dry key release leaves a faint, pitched afterimage that disperses across the room. Check a fast chord change, mono fold-down and silence after the tail.
4. **Author and select three signature sounds.** One intimate acoustic pad, one wide glass landscape and one dark unstable pad should each justify a different feature. Map a small set of named macros per sound. Audition a single note, an open chord with hands off the controls, and a phrase with one continuous gesture. Test fresh ears on matched recordings before expanding the preset library.

Keep audio-rate work in C and instrument definitions in external JSON/Lua. Prepare all curves, tables and samples away from the audio callback. Benchmark at the existing desktop and Quest voice budgets after every stage. Grow the sample pool only as measured peak memory and reload continuity allow.
